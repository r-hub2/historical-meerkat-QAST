Joint regression of survival and quality-adjusted survival with QAST

Quality-adjusted survival

Survival time alone treats every month of life the same. In many studies, especially in oncology, the time a patient spends with treatment toxicity or after a relapse is worth less than time in good health. Quality-adjusted survival weights each period of life by a utility between 0 and 1, so the quality-adjusted time qt of a subject is never larger than the survival time t.

Censoring is harder here than in ordinary survival analysis: subjects with a poor quality of life accumulate quality-adjusted time slowly, so censoring becomes informative on that scale. QAST handles this with inverse probability of censoring weights (IPCW), estimated by Kaplan-Meier or by a Cox model.

QAST fits bivariate semiparametric accelerated failure time (AFT) models for t and qt jointly:

  • semi_aft_fg(): the model of Fine and Gelber (2001), for binary covariates, with truncation at a time L;
  • semi_aft_prop(): the model proposed in the doctoral thesis of da Silva (2026), for continuous or discrete covariates.

Each fit has two components: beta for the survival time and theta for the quality-adjusted survival time.

Data

Each subject contributes an observed time t, a quality-adjusted time qt (with 0 <= qt <= t), an event indicator status (1 = event, 0 = censored) and covariates. We simulate a small study:

library(QAST)

set.seed(2026)
n <- 150
d <- data.frame(
  trt = rbinom(n, 1, 0.5),
  sex = rbinom(n, 1, 0.4),
  age = round(rnorm(n, 60, 8))
)
t_event <- rexp(n, rate = exp(-0.4 * d$trt))   # treatment lengthens survival
t_cens  <- rexp(n, rate = 0.3)
d$status <- as.integer(t_event <= t_cens)
d$t  <- pmin(t_event, t_cens)
d$qt <- d$t * runif(n, 0.5, 1)                  # utility between 0.5 and 1
head(d)

The response is written with Qsurv(time, qtime, status) on the left-hand side of a formula, like survival::Surv(). The models do not estimate an intercept (their estimating equations are invariant to shifts), and factors enter as indicators.

Fine and Gelber model

The Fine and Gelber model needs binary covariates and a truncation time L, usually a time with enough follow-up left, such as an upper quantile of the observed times:

fit_fg <- semi_aft_fg(Qsurv(t, qt, status) ~ trt + sex, data = d,
                      L = quantile(d$t, 0.9), var_comp = TRUE)

Proposed model

semi_aft_prop() accepts continuous covariates:

fit_prop <- semi_aft_prop(Qsurv(t, qt, status) ~ age + trt, data = d,
                          var_comp = TRUE)
coef(fit_prop)
#>           beta       theta
#> age 0.00347012 -0.01202984
#> trt 0.66051566  0.39909277
vcov(fit_prop, "theta")
#>              age        trt
#> age 0.0006624136 0.00258076
#> trt 0.0025807599 0.13939413

coef() returns a matrix with the beta and theta columns; vcov() returns the covariance matrix of one component.

Intercepts

The rank-based estimating equations do not change when every residual is shifted by the same constant, so they do not identify an intercept. Each fit estimates it afterwards as the mean of the distribution of the residuals of its component:

  • survival time: the mean of the Kaplan-Meier curve of \(h_1(T_i) - Z_i^\top\hat\beta\) (Ding and Nan, 2015);
  • quality-adjusted time: the IPCW mean \(n^{-1}\sum_i \delta_i r_i / \hat G(T_i)\) of \(r_i = h_2(Q_i) - Z_i^\top\hat\theta\) (Zhao and Tsiatis, 2000), the mean of the distribution estimated by surv_qt_fun().
fit_prop$intercept
#>       beta      theta 
#> -0.9542239 -0.3226268
# Predicted log survival time and log quality-adjusted time
z <- model.matrix(~ age + trt, d)[, -1]
head(cbind(
  log_t  = fit_prop$intercept[["beta"]]  + drop(z %*% fit_prop$beta),
  log_qt = fit_prop$intercept[["theta"]] + drop(z %*% fit_prop$theta)
))
#>         log_t     log_qt
#> 1 -0.08550100 -0.6453243
#> 2 -0.07509064 -0.6814138
#> 3 -0.71825570 -1.1406557
#> 4 -0.73213618 -1.0925364
#> 5 -0.06815040 -0.7054735
#> 6 -0.71478558 -1.1526856

The intercepts have no standard errors and are not part of coef() or vcov().

Predictions

predict() returns the linear predictors (type = "lp", intercept included) or the survival probabilities given the covariates (type = "survival"). Since the error does not depend on the covariates, \(S(t \mid z) = S_e\{h(t) - z^\top\hat\beta\}\): the Kaplan-Meier curve of the residuals for the survival time and the Zhao and Tsiatis (1997) estimator of the residuals for the quality-adjusted time.

new <- data.frame(age = c(0, 0), trt = c(0, 1))
predict(fit_prop, new)
#>         beta       theta
#> 1 -0.9542239 -0.32262679
#> 2 -0.2937082  0.07646597
tg <- seq(0.1, 3, length.out = 60)
s <- predict(fit_prop, new, type = "survival", times = tg)
matplot(tg, t(s$theta), type = "s", lty = 1:2, col = 1, ylim = c(0, 1),
        xlab = "Quality-adjusted survival time",
        ylab = "Estimated survival probability")
legend("topright", c("trt = 0", "trt = 1"), lty = 1:2, bty = "n")

Model checking

qast_pp() draws the P-P plots of the residuals of Fine and Gelber (2001, Section 4). Under the model the distribution of the residuals does not depend on the covariates, so the distributions estimated in two covariate groups should lie close to the diagonal. Check the survival time component (a) first: the quality-adjusted diagnostic (b) assumes it is correct. In the Fine and Gelber model, (b) is the conditional distribution of the quality-adjusted residuals given the trimming required by the estimating function of \(\theta\). Continuous covariates are discretized to form the groups:

pp <- qast_pp(fit_prop, group = cut(d$age, c(-Inf, median(d$age), Inf),
                                    labels = c("younger", "older")))
pp
#> 
#> P-P plots of the residuals (Fine and Gelber, 2001)
#> Groups: younger, older (reference: younger)
#> Events in the quality-adjusted distribution: younger = 52, older = 50
#> Largest vertical distance to the diagonal:
#>       older
#> beta  0.152
#> theta 0.118
#> Use plot() to draw them.
plot(pp)

Variance

Standard errors come from the delete-1 jackknife (var_method = "jackknife", the default) or the bootstrap (var_method = "bootstrap", with M replicates). The replicates run in parallel on the physical cores; options(QAST.cores = k) sets the number of processes.

Survival curve of the quality-adjusted time

surv_qt_fun() implements the estimator of Zhao and Tsiatis (1997) for the survival curve of the quality-adjusted time: each uncensored subject is weighted by the inverse of the Kaplan-Meier censoring survival at its survival time, which corrects the induced informative censoring:

s_qt <- surv_qt_fun(qaly = d$qt, time = d$t, status = d$status)
ord <- order(d$qt)
plot(d$qt[ord], s_qt[ord], type = "s", ylim = c(0, 1),
     xlab = "Quality-adjusted survival time",
     ylab = "Estimated survival probability")
